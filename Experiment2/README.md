# Experiment 2: Controlling Local Disparity Cues in 3D Slant Perception

## Overview

Experiment 2 extended Experiment 1 by examining whether the observed effects of sensory reliability on perceived 3D slant could be explained by local disparity information rather than perceived surface slant.

In Experiment 1, local disparity magnitudes varied systematically with surface slant, potentially allowing participants to perform the discrimination task using disparities at specific surface locations.

To address this potential confound, Experiment 2 introduced trial-to-trial perturbations in the simulated distance of the planar surface during slant discrimination, thereby increasing variability in absolute disparity while preserving surface slant.

## Participants

Thirty-eight participants (24 women; mean age = 22.2 ± 2.1 years) were recruited.

Two participants were excluded because their slant estimates showed insufficient sensitivity to simulated slant, and four additional participants discontinued before completing the experiment.

The final sample consisted of **32 participants**.

All included participants had normal or corrected-to-normal vision and passed clinical and computer-based stereoacuity screening.

## Apparatus and Stimuli

The apparatus, stimuli, and experimental conditions were identical to those used in Experiment 1.

- **Viewing distances:** 90 and 180 cm.
- **Slant estimation:** Eight simulated slants from 0° to 70°, in 10° increments.
- **Slant discrimination:** Two standard slants, 30° and 60°.
- **Cue conditions:** Texture-only, stereo-only, cue-consistent, and cue-conflict.

### Critical Manipulation: Simulated Distance Perturbation

In the slant discrimination task, the simulated distance of the planar surface was randomly perturbed on each trial:

| Nominal viewing distance | Distance perturbation |
|---|---|
| 90 cm | ±12 cm |
| 180 cm | ±24 cm |

Perturbations were sampled from uniform distributions, corresponding to approximately ±13% of the nominal viewing distance.

This manipulation increased trial-to-trial variability in absolute disparity without changing the simulated surface slant, reducing the usefulness of local disparity cues for performing the discrimination task.

## Experimental Tasks

### Slant Estimation

Participants adjusted their right-hand orientation to match the perceived slant of simulated planar surfaces.

The procedure was identical to Experiment 1, with **1,344 trials per participant** across four sessions.

### Slant Discrimination

Participants judged which of two sequentially presented surfaces appeared more slanted.

The procedure was identical to Experiment 1, except that the simulated surface distance was randomly perturbed on each trial.

An adaptive minimum expected entropy procedure was used to estimate discrimination thresholds and points of subjective equality.

Each participant completed **1,440 slant discrimination trials** across four sessions.

## Research Objectives

1. Reduce the potential contribution of local disparity cues to slant discrimination.
2. Determine whether discrimination performance reflects sensitivity to perceived global surface slant.
3. Examine whether reliability-dependent perceptual biases and nonlinearities persist under simulated distance perturbations.
4. Evaluate whether sensory reliabilities measured under these conditions can predict slant estimates within a Bayesian framework.

## Data and Analysis

The analyses followed the same procedures as Experiment 1, focusing on:

- Slant estimation responses and perceptual gains.
- Slant discrimination thresholds.
- Effects of viewing distance and cue availability.
- Reliability-dependent changes in stereo and texture cue integration.
- Bayesian model predictions of perceived slant.

## Relationship to Experiment 1

Experiment 1 examined the relationship between sensory reliability and perceived slant. Experiment 2 tested the robustness of this relationship by reducing the usefulness of local disparity cues during discrimination.

Together, the two experiments evaluate whether systematic variations in slant perception are consistent with reliability-dependent Bayesian cue integration rather than fixed biases in the interpretation of individual visual cues.
