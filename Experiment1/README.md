# Experiment 1: Sensory Cue Reliability and 3D Slant Perception

## Overview

Experiment 1 investigated how the reliability of stereoscopic and texture information influences perceived three-dimensional (3D) surface slant. The experiment examined whether perceptual biases and nonlinearities could be explained by a Bayesian model in which sensory information is integrated with a prior favoring frontoparallel surfaces.

## Participants

Thirty-one participants (23 women; mean age = 22.1 ± 1.8 years) were recruited. Six participants were excluded because their slant estimates showed insufficient sensitivity to changes in simulated slant (regression slope below 0.1 in at least one experimental condition), resulting in a final sample of **25 participants**.

All included participants had normal or corrected-to-normal vision and passed clinical and computer-based stereoacuity screening.

## Apparatus and Stimuli

Stimuli were computer-generated slanted planar surfaces presented on a 27-inch stereoscopic LCD monitor (1920 × 1080, 120 Hz) using NVIDIA 3D Vision 2 shutter glasses.

The experiment included:

- **Viewing distances:** 90 and 180 cm.
- **Slant estimation:** Eight simulated slants from 0° to 70°, in 10° increments.
- **Slant discrimination:** Two standard slants, 30° and 60°.
- **Cue conditions:**
  - Texture-only: Monocular Voronoi texture.
  - Stereo-only: Binocular random-dot stereograms.
  - Cue-consistent: Texture and stereo specifying the same slant.
  - Cue-conflict: Texture and stereo specifying different slants.

All surfaces were slanted about a horizontal axis and viewed through a circular, frontoparallel aperture subtending 10° of visual angle.

## Experimental Tasks

### Slant Estimation

Participants adjusted their right-hand orientation to match the perceived slant of a simulated surface. Hand orientation was recorded using an inclinometer and calibrated using a separate hand-alignment task.

Each participant completed **1,344 slant estimation trials** across four sessions.

### Slant Discrimination

Participants viewed two sequentially presented surfaces and judged whether the second surface appeared steeper or flatter than the first.

An adaptive minimum expected entropy procedure was used to estimate discrimination thresholds and points of subjective equality.

Each participant completed **1,440 slant discrimination trials** across four sessions.

## Research Objectives

1. Determine how viewing distance influences the reliability of stereoscopic slant information.
2. Examine how sensory reliability affects perceptual gain and nonlinearities in slant estimation.
3. Estimate stereo and texture cue reliabilities from discrimination performance.
4. Evaluate whether a Bayesian model incorporating sensory reliability and a frontal-surface prior can predict perceived slant.

## Data and Analysis

The data support analyses of:

- Perceived slant as a function of simulated slant.
- Perceptual gain across viewing distances and cue conditions.
- Slant discrimination thresholds.
- Relative contributions of stereo and texture information.
- Bayesian model predictions of perceptual gain and slant estimates.
